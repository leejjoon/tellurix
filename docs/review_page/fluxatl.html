telluric-corrected = observed ÷ model × stellar model, blanked where the modelled transmission falls below 0.15. Compare it with the stellar model: what is left is the solar model’s own error.
the authors’ correction is Kurucz’s 2005 residual irradiance atlas (irradrelwl.dat; Kurucz 2006): the same scans, re-reduced, with a telluric spectrum computed from HITRAN, its line parameters adjusted by hand, divided out. Where that failed he drew straight lines across the spectrum; those stretches are blank here. It was released Gaussian smoothed at three times each scan’s dispersion and resampled to 0.001 nm, so it is smoother than ours by construction and is drawn through a cubic interpolation. It is moved by the velocity that best aligns it with ours where nothing absorbs (about −0.1 km/s) and put on our scale by a straight line fitted there. “Kurucz’s implied T” is observed ÷ his spectrum. His corrected flux is drawn only where both transmissions clear 0.15. Scan 115 (above 1000 nm) was never re-reduced and has no authors’ columns.
residual / pixel noise — the shaded band is ±1σ. The noise is 0.02–0.04% of continuum, so the ~1–2% the solar model misses reads as 50–100σ: judge a window by its rms in % and by the flatness of the corrected spectrum, not by σ.
