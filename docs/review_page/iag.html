telluric-corrected = observed ÷ model × stellar model, blanked where the modelled transmission falls below 0.15. Compare it with the stellar model: what is left is the solar model’s own error.
there is no authors’ correction of this atlas. Drawn instead is Baker, Blake & Reiners 2020’s telluric-free IAG Sun — the same instrument, 2015–16 spectra, the atmosphere removed by an empirical per-line fit over air mass — moved by the velocity that best aligns it with ours where nothing absorbs, and put on our scale by a straight line fitted there. “Baker’s implied T” is observed ÷ that spectrum: the transmission their Sun says this atlas saw. Theirs is sampled at about one point per resolution element and is drawn through a cubic interpolation, so narrow-line differences of a few tenths of a percent are interpolation, not either correction (the score samples ours on their grid instead). Their corrected flux is drawn only where both transmissions clear 0.15. Effective T and the implied T share the right-hand axis.
residual / pixel noise — the shaded band is ±1σ. The noise is 0.02–0.03% of continuum, so the ~1–2% the solar model misses reads as 50–100σ: judge a window by its rms in % and by the flatness of the corrected spectrum, not by σ.
